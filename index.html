The Long Wait

Formula 1's only ever-present team. Its most successful, by almost every measure ever kept. And yet, for more than a decade and a half, Ferrari has been chasing a drivers' title it once seemed to own outright.

The Only Constant

Every grid since the beginning has had a Ferrari on it.

Formula 1 began in 1950, and Ferrari was there. Marques have come and gone by the dozen since — great names and one-season hopefuls alike — but through seventy-five seasons of rule changes, eras and upheavals, one scarlet constant has never missed a championship. No other team can say it.

It is not merely longevity. By the records that matter most, Ferrari sits atop the sport: more constructors' championships than anyone, more drivers' titles than any other team has delivered, and a tally of race wins and pole positions no rival has approached. The Prancing Horse is, statistically, the most successful team in the history of grand prix racing.

Which makes the silence of recent years so loud. The team that wrote the record book has spent the modern era waiting — and the wait is the story.

1950

The beginning, and a beginning that never ended.

When the World Championship was first contested in 1950, Ferrari entered. It has never stopped — the only team to appear, unbroken, in every season since.

2000 — 2004

The golden age: five straight drivers' crowns.

With Michael Schumacher leading a relentless team, Ferrari won five consecutive drivers' championships and dominated the constructors' standings. For a while, the record book simply belonged to Maranello.

2007 — 2008

The last titles — closer than anyone remembers.

Kimi Räikkönen took the 2007 drivers' championship by a single point. In 2008, Ferrari claimed the constructors' title. No one knew these would be the last for a very long time.

2009 — today

And then, the gap.

The seasons kept coming. The titles did not. What began as a lull hardened into the longest championship drought of Ferrari's modern history — the long wait that defines the team today.

1999 — 2004

What total dominance actually looked like.

This is the shape of an empire. Through the Schumacher years, Ferrari didn't just win championships — it won races by the bucketload, season after season.

2002 & 2004

Fifteen wins in a single season — twice.

In both 2002 and 2004, Ferrari won fifteen grands prix in one year, two of the most commanding campaigns the sport has ever seen. The drivers' title was effectively decided before the calendar ran out.

The benchmark

A high-water mark that still casts a shadow.

Every season since has been measured, fairly or not, against these numbers. They are the reason the wait feels so long: Ferrari has been here before, at the very top, and remembers exactly how it felt.

Decided by 1 point.

2007

The last drivers' title came down to a single point.

Heading into the final race of 2007, Räikkönen was the outsider. He won the season finale — and the championship — finishing on 110 points to the 109 of both McLaren drivers.

One point

The thinnest possible margin.

One point separated triumph from heartbreak. It remains Ferrari's most recent drivers' championship — and a reminder of how fine the line at the very top can be.

2008

One more, then the door closed.

Ferrari followed up with the constructors' title in 2008. After that, the championship column went quiet — and the counting of years began.

2008 — today

Then the years began to add up.

Since that 2007 drivers' crown, the count has only grown. For a team that once won five titles in a row, every season without one has weighed a little heavier than the last.

2010 & 2012

So close, with Alonso.

Fernando Alonso carried Ferrari to the brink twice — losing the championship at the final round in both 2010 and 2012, each time by a handful of points. Maranello has rarely been closer.

2017 & 2018

And again, with Vettel.

Sebastian Vettel led the standings deep into both the 2017 and 2018 seasons before the title slipped away. Strong starts, familiar endings — the pattern of the long wait.

The Year the Wait Ends?

A seven-time champion and a homegrown hope.

Ferrari now fields one of the most talked-about line-ups in its history: Lewis Hamilton, a seven-time world champion, alongside Charles Leclerc, the Monégasque the team has built its future around. Between them they carry enormous expectation — and the weight of every year on the counter.
